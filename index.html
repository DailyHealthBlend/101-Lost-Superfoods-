Ancient Grains & Seeds · No. 01

Amaranth

A sacred crop of the Aztecs, grown on a huge scale in Mesoamerica long before European contact. Its tiny seeds cook into a soft, slightly nutty porridge and pop like miniature popcorn in a dry pan.

Gluten-Free Pantry Staple Easy to Find
5 minPrep Time
20 minCook Time
2Servings
EasyDifficulty

🥣 Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup amaranth
  • 1 1/4 cups water (1 part amaranth to 2.5 parts water)
  • 1 apple, sliced
  • 1/2 tsp cinnamon
  • 1 drizzle of honey
  • Pinch of salt

🔧 Equipment Needed

  • Small saucepan with a lid
  • Wooden spoon
  • Measuring cups
  • Optional: dry skillet for popping

👩‍🍳 Instructions

  1. Add the amaranth, water and a pinch of salt to a saucepan and bring to a boil.
  2. Reduce the heat, cover and simmer for about 20 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and creamy.
  3. Stir in the cinnamon and sliced apple for the last few minutes.
  4. Spoon into bowls and finish with a drizzle of honey.

🌱 Nutrition Highlights

  • About 9 g protein per cooked cup
  • Good source of manganese and magnesium
  • Provides iron and dietary fibre
  • Naturally gluten-free

🛒 Where to Buy

  • USA/Canada: Whole Foods, Bob's Red Mill, Amazon
  • UK: Holland & Barrett, Waitrose, Ocado
  • Often in the gluten-free or health aisle

💡 Pro Tips

  • Cook a big batch on Sunday and reheat through the week.
  • Pop a spoonful in a hot dry pan for a crunchy topping.
  • Stir into soups and stews to thicken them.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using too little water, which leaves the porridge gluey and stuck to the pan.
  • Leaving the lid off for the whole cook, so it dries out before it softens.
  • Walking away completely. A quick stir keeps it creamy.

🫙 Storage & Reheating

  • Dry amaranth: keep in an airtight jar in a cool, dark cupboard for up to 6 months, or in the fridge or freezer for longer.
  • Cooked porridge: refrigerate in a sealed container and reheat gently on the stove with a splash of water or milk, stirring until creamy.
